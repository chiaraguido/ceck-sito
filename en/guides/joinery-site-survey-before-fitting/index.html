Joinery site survey: what to check before fitting furniture

The unit is made and the client is waiting. The fitters arrive on site and find a socket right behind a cabinet. Or that the wall is out of square. A proper joinery site survey catches these things first.

These are problems that cost hours of alterations, sometimes a part to remake. A well-documented site survey, before production, avoids almost all of them.

Don't just take measurements

Width, height and depth are not enough. During the survey also check:

  • the position of sockets;
  • switches and light points;
  • where the natural light comes from;
  • pipework and radiators;
  • skirting boards and projections;
  • walls out of square and out of plumb;
  • access to the room: stairs, doors, lift, how the parts get in;
  • anything that could prevent fitting: other trades still working, walls to finish, floor not laid.

Take photos before you forget

An overall photo of the room and a few detail shots help you find information that becomes important back in the workshop, when the client isn't there to ask.

But a photo with no location and no description is hard to understand two weeks later: which wall was it? From which side?

Sketch the room on site

A floor plan with measurements, notes and the points you took the photos from lets you rebuild the room even once you are back in the workshop. Whoever makes it and whoever fits it look at the same thing, with no phone calls asking "where was that socket?".

In short

  • ✅ Measurements, but also sockets, pipes, radiators, skirting and out-of-square walls
  • ✅ Access to the room and obstructions checked beforehand
  • ✅ Overall and detail photos, each with location and description
  • ✅ Floor plan with measurements and photo points, available in the workshop and at fitting

Before you build a piece of furniture, make sure you know the place it has to fit.
