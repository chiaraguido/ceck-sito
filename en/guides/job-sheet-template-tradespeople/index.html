Job sheet: how to record the work you did at a client’s

An electrician replaces a socket.

A plumber fixes a leak.

A joiner goes back to a client to adjust a door.

They look like simple jobs, but they still need to be recorded.

What should a job sheet include?

A working record should show:

  • the client’s details;
  • the job address;
  • the date;
  • the problem reported;
  • the work carried out;
  • the hours spent;
  • the materials used;
  • anything still to be done.

Where possible, it’s worth keeping photos of the problem and of the finished result too.

A photo can save a lot of arguments

Say you’re repairing a damaged unit.

Before you start, you photograph the damage. Afterwards, you photograph the result.

If the client calls you back a few weeks later, it’s much easier to show what you did.

When the job needs a second visit

You can’t always sort everything out on the spot.

You may need to order a part, wait for another trade or get the client’s go-ahead.

Recording the problem means you won’t forget what’s still to do.
