Digital site diary: how to keep a record of the works without losing information

Do you remember exactly what happened on site three weeks ago?

Who fitted that door? When did the materials arrive? Why was that job stopped?

When you run a site, relying on memory alone can become a problem.

What is a site diary?

It’s a tool for recording how the work is going, every day.

An internal site record can include:

  • who was on site;
  • hours worked;
  • work carried out;
  • materials delivered;
  • photos;
  • delays and the unexpected;
  • problems to fix.

An internal record doesn’t necessarily replace a site diary required by law or by your contract.

Why go from paper to digital?

A notebook can help, but when you need something from two months ago, finding it gets hard.

With a digital tool the information is organised by date, job, room and task.

Photos need to mean something

Taking a photo of a problem isn’t enough.

You need to know where it is, when it was found, who has to fix it and whether it has really been fixed.

With ceck you link the photo to its position on the drawing and follow it until it’s fixed.

Don’t wait for the end of the day

The best time to record something is when it happens.

Materials arrive? Log them.

A task is finished? Update the progress.

Found a defect? Photograph it and assign it.

What you record during the day goes into the daily site report automatically.

Build your site’s memory too

A site isn’t made only of materials and labour, but of information too.

Keeping it organised means you can piece together what happened, even long afterwards.
