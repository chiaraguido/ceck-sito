Tiling jobs: how to organise the job before, during and after tiling

A tiler can work fast and with great precision. But on tiling jobs, if the substrate isn't ready, the whole schedule falls apart.

Check before you start

Before tiling, check:

  • the condition of the substrate;
  • flatness and levels;
  • that the tiles have arrived, in the right quantity and batch;
  • adhesive, grout and consumables;
  • the position of the wastes;
  • other trades in the same room;
  • drawings and setting-out instructions.

Technical checks must be carried out according to the job specification and the applicable rules.

Record the starting conditions

Photograph the substrate before you start and write down any problems you find: cracks, uneven levels, damp. It is proof of how you found the room, useful if someone disputes the result later.

Hours by room

A bathroom, a hallway and a large living room take very different tiling times. If you log hours separately by room, you know what each type of work really costs you and you quote more accurately next time.

Final touch-ups

A chipped tile, a grout line to finish, a movement joint to correct: write them down as snags to do before handover, with photo and location. That way you don't forget them when the client does the final walk-round.

In short

  • ✅ Substrate, levels, materials and wastes checked beforehand
  • ✅ Photos of the starting conditions
  • ✅ Hours logged separately by room
  • ✅ Touch-ups written down with photo and location

The quality of the tiling also depends on the quality of the organisation.
