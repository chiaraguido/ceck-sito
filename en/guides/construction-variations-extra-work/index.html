Construction variations and extra work: how to stop working for free

"While you're here, could you sort this out too?" Every tradesperson knows that line. It is how most construction variations and extra work start.

The problem comes at the end of the job, when you have to invoice all the small extras asked for along the way and nobody wrote them down. Variations are normal on every job: the problem is not doing them, it is not recording them.

What counts as a variation

A variation is anything that was not in the quote or the contract:

  • a change asked for by the client;
  • extra work found during the job;
  • work to put right damage done by others;
  • hours lost because of hold-ups that are not your fault.

Record it before you do it

When you are asked for extra work, agree it and write it down straight away:

  • who asked for it and when;
  • where: the room and the exact spot;
  • what needs doing, with a photo before you start;
  • which materials are needed;
  • the price or rate agreed.

Whenever you can, get a written variation order before you start.

"Extra work in the kitchen" is no use to anyone. "Kitchen, move socket on north wall 60 cm, requested by the client on 12/11, photos before and after" goes on the invoice without an argument.

Hours and materials kept separate

Hours and materials for extra work must be logged separately from the work in the quote. If they end up in the same total, at the end you no longer know what the variation cost you and you have no figures to show the client.

The client's confirmation

Before you start, or straight after if it is urgent, get the client to confirm the work: an email, a message or a signature, with the date. One written confirmation is worth more than ten phone calls.

Photos before and after

The photo before shows why the work was needed. The photo after shows it was done. Logging the hours by room and task on the same day also helps you work out later what happened and when: see the guide to construction timesheets.

In short

  • ✅ Every variation written down on the same day: who, when, where, what
  • ✅ Hours and materials kept separate from the quoted work
  • ✅ Written confirmation from the client
  • ✅ Photos before and after

One five-minute job can be a favour. A hundred five-minute jobs are more than a full day's work.
