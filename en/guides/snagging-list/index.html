Snagging list: how to manage snags and outstanding works on site

A door that won't close, a chipped tile, a missing socket, a wall that needs touching up. On a building site there are dozens of small snags, and different trades have to fix them. A snagging list keeps them all in one place.

Remembering them all is impossible. And every forgotten snag turns up at the worst moment: at handover, in front of the client. Good snag management starts with one simple rule: every problem must be found, assigned and checked.

Why a photo on WhatsApp is not enough

"There's a defect near the door." Which door? On which floor? The photo shows the problem, but it does not say:

  • where it was taken;
  • who has to fix it;
  • whether it has already been fixed;
  • whether anyone has checked it was done properly.

Two weeks later the photo is buried under a hundred messages, and nobody remembers if it was sorted.

What a snag report should contain

Every snag, which on many sites is also called a remark, needs six pieces of information:

  • 📍 the spot on the floor plan;
  • 📷 a photo, ideally showing the surroundings so you can tell where it is;
  • ✏️ a short description: "cracked filler above the door frame", not "needs fixing";
  • 👷 who fixes it: the person or the subcontractor;
  • ⏳ the status: to do, done, checked;
  • ✅ the check: who checked it and when.

Each trade gets its own list

The window fitter needs to see window snags, the electrician the electrical ones. Sending everyone the whole site list means each of them scrolls through it, can't find theirs and rings to ask.

When each subcontractor gets only their own snags, with photo and location, they come to site once and already know what to bring.

"Done" does not mean "checked"

A job marked as finished is not necessarily a job done properly. Before handover you need a checking round: go back through the snags marked as done, look at them on site and close each one only when it really is right.

Site tip: do the checking round with the floor plan in hand, room by room, not by following the list. That way you miss nothing and don't double back three times.

In short

  • ✅ Every snag with a photo, a spot on the floor plan and a clear description
  • ✅ One person responsible for each fix
  • ✅ Each subcontractor sees only their own snags
  • ✅ Closed only after checking on site
