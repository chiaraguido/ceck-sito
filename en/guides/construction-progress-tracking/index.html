Construction progress: do you really know where your job stands?

The client asks you when the work will be finished.

You call the foreman. The foreman calls the worker. The worker tells you there’s still something left.

But how much is left, exactly?

Saying a job is nearly finished isn’t enough

A room can look finished and still need a socket fitted, a skirting board put on and a door adjusted.

Small jobs like these can stop the whole room being handed over.

That’s why you need to tell apart work started, work done and work actually finished.

How to track real progress

For each task it helps to know:

  • the planned start date;
  • the planned finish date;
  • the percentage complete;
  • anything holding it up;
  • what’s still to do;
  • the problems to fix.

This lets you spot delays before they become hard to recover.

Why is the work late?

Not every delay is down to the workers.

Materials may be missing. Another trade may not have finished. The client may have asked for a change.

Knowing why it’s late matters as much as knowing that it’s late.
