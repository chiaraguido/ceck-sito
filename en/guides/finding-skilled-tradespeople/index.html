Finding skilled tradespeople: “joiner” on a CV isn’t enough

You need a joiner. They send you a joiner.

On paper, at least.

Maybe he has twenty years’ experience making wooden pallets. But when you ask him to hang a door, fit two skirting boards and make an architrave with perfect joints, you find his experience doesn’t match what you need.

It doesn’t necessarily mean he’s a bad joiner. It means he’s the wrong person for that job.

Same trade, completely different skills

A joiner might specialise in factory production, furniture making, kitchen fitting, door hanging or high-end fit-outs.

They work the same material, but with different skills, tools and levels of precision.

The same goes for bricklayers, electricians, welders, painters and tilers.

And yet, when we look for staff, we still often use one word for all of them.

What the wrong person costs you on site

Say you give a worker a task that should take eight hours.

It takes sixteen and the result isn’t good enough. A second worker has to put it right, taking another eight hours.

Work priced8 h
First attempt16 h
Putting it right8 h
Actual total24 h
Extra hours16 h

At a cost to the firm of €30 an hour, you’ve spent €480 more.

Not counting wasted materials, the delay and the argument with the client.

Years of experience don’t tell the whole story

Someone with twenty years’ experience isn’t necessarily a better fit than someone with five.

The right question isn’t only: how long have you been doing this?

You should ask:

  • Which jobs can you do on your own?
  • Which materials do you know?
  • Which tools can you use?
  • Can you read a technical drawing?
  • What standard of finish are you used to?
  • Can you work in furnished, lived-in homes?
  • Have you worked on high-end jobs?

What makes the difference is the match between the person’s skills and what the job needs.
