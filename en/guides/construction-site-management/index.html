Construction site management: how to keep control of hours, work and costs

Construction site management is not just sending the lads to work. It means knowing every evening what got done, what it cost, what is missing and what could stop you tomorrow.

The problem is almost never willingness to work. It is that the information is scattered. Hours on a sheet in the van, photos on an apprentice's phone, materials in a WhatsApp message, snags in the site manager's head. When you need a figure, you can't find it. Good construction site management rests on five points. Here they are.

1. Plan the day before it starts

At 7:30 every operative must know where they are working, what they are doing and what comes first. If they find out on site, the first half hour is gone, and with a gang of five that is two and a half paid hours a day.

  • Prepare the plan the evening before, not in the morning at the café.
  • Write down priorities: if it rains, who moves inside and what they do.
  • Avoid two trades in the same room: the drylining fitter and the electrician in the same room hold each other up.

A weekly programme everyone can see, even on their phone, cuts half the morning phone calls.

2. Track hours: not how many, but where

Knowing that an operative did 8 hours is not enough. You need to know on which task: 8 hours of skimming on the second floor, or 8 hours looking for materials?

Example. You allowed 40 hours for the first-floor wall linings. After three days you have already used 35 and you are halfway. If you spot it now, you can act: add people to the gang, find out what is slowing things down, talk to the client. If you spot it at the end of the job, those hours are already lost.

Comparing hours allowed / hours used for every task is the most useful check to avoid working at a loss. We explain how to do it step by step, and how to fill in timesheets without wasting time, in construction timesheets: how to track labour hours.

3. Snags: photo, location, person

"There's a defect near the door." Which door? On which floor? Who has to go? A report with no photo and no location means three phone calls and often a wasted trip.

Every snag needs three things:

  • a photo of the problem;
  • the spot on the floor plan, so there is no doubt where it is;
  • the name of whoever fixes it, and a tick when it is done and checked.

4. Materials: spot it the day before

Finding out at 8am that you are six bags of adhesive short means stopping a gang or sending someone to the merchant for half the morning. Someone had almost always seen the shortage coming. The problem is they did not write it down anywhere.

  • Whoever sees something running low logs it straight away, from their phone.
  • At the end of the day the site manager has the list ready and orders for the next day.

5. The daily site report without taking it home

The daily site report should not be extra work done at home in the evening, from memory. If hours, tasks, photos and materials were logged during the day, the report is already written: it just needs checking and signing.

In short: the site manager's checklist

  • ✅ Day plan ready the evening before
  • ✅ Hours logged by task, compared with hours allowed
  • ✅ Every snag with a photo, a spot on the floor plan and a person responsible
  • ✅ Missing materials logged as soon as they are spotted
  • ✅ Daily site report filled in from the day's data
